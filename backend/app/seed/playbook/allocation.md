# Allocation

Allocation is how the bonds are shared among the investors who placed orders. When a book is oversubscribed, not every order can be filled in full, so the allocation has to be fair, explainable and agreed with the issuer.

## Allocation principles

The principles are agreed with the issuer before the book closes. Typical priorities are long-term real-money investors, investors who placed orders early or gave useful feedback during pre-marketing, and the mix of investor types and regions that the issuer wants. For a green or sustainability-linked bond, investors with a sustainability mandate may be favoured. Orders that look inflated are not rewarded.

## Scale-back and rounding

If demand is larger than the deal, orders are scaled back. This can be done in proportion to the order size inside each priority group. Amounts are rounded to the minimum denomination and increment set in the terms of the bond, so a small remainder is given out by a documented rule. The total allocated must equal the final deal size exactly.

## Four-eyes control

One person prepares the allocation proposal and a different person checks and approves it. The issuer then approves the final allocation. Both the proposal and the approval are recorded in the audit trail. In this application the person who proposed an allocation cannot approve it.

## Communication

Allocations are communicated to investors, usually on the same day as pricing. Investors confirm that they accept them.

## Exit criteria

The principles are agreed, the proposal is prepared and checked, the issuer approves and the allocations have been communicated.
