# Pricing

Pricing is where the issuer and the lead managers fix the final terms of the bond, based on how strong the order book was.

## Setting the final price

The final spread over the benchmark, often mid-swaps for a euro bond, is agreed with the issuer. From that spread and the market rates at the time, the yield, the coupon and the issue price are worked out. The coupon is normally a rounded figure, and the issue price is set so that the yield matches the agreed spread. The final spread and coupon are a blocking task.

## The pricing term sheet

After pricing, the syndicate desk releases a pricing term sheet to investors. It lists the final size, the coupon, the issue price, the spread, the settlement date and other key terms. The ISIN is normally included or follows shortly after.

## Final terms

Legal counsel signs off the final terms. The final terms must match the pricing term sheet exactly, because the booking is later checked against them. A mismatch found at this point is cheap to fix and a mismatch found after settlement is expensive.

## Timing

Pricing usually happens on the same day the book closes. Allocation follows immediately, and the bond becomes free to trade once the allocations have been confirmed.

## Exit criteria

The final spread and coupon are agreed, the pricing term sheet is released and the final terms are signed off by legal.
