# Book building

Book building is the period in which investors place orders and the syndicate desk collects, checks and reports them. The result decides the final size and the final price.

## Reporting to the issuer

At agreed intervals the syndicate desk updates the issuer on the state of the book: the total order amount, how it is split by investor type and by region, and how price sensitive the orders are. Regular updates help the issuer decide how aggressively to tighten the price.

## Pricing guidance

As demand builds, the guidance is usually tightened from the initial price thoughts to a narrower range and finally to a final guidance level. The spread moving in during the day, which is called price tension, shows strong demand. Each revision is agreed with the issuer and communicated to investors.

## Quality of the book

A large cover ratio alone does not mean a good book. The desk checks the quality of orders. It looks for duplicate orders from the same investor, inflated orders where an investor asks for much more than it wants in the hope of a larger allocation, orders from the managers' own accounts and orders that are far above an investor's normal size. It also looks at the share of real-money investors, such as asset managers, insurers and pension funds, who are long-term holders. Order quality checks are a blocking task.

## Closing the book

When the guidance is final and demand is clear, the books are closed, often after a short warning to investors. The issuer and the leads agree the final size. Closing the book and agreeing the size are blocking tasks.

## Exit criteria

The quality checks are complete, the book is closed and the final size is agreed with the issuer.
