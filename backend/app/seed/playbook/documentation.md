# Documentation

In the documentation stage, lawyers and the deal team prepare the legal documents that describe the bond and the offer. The stage ends when the documents are approved or agreed and the deal can be marketed.

## Programme and prospectus

Many issuers sell bonds under a debt issuance programme, such as a Euro Medium Term Note programme, with a base prospectus that is updated about once a year. A single bond under the programme is then documented with final terms and does not need a new prospectus. A stand-alone bond needs its own prospectus or offering document. Where the base prospectus is updated, the competent regulator must approve it before it can be used.

## Deal-specific documents

The draft final terms set out the commercial terms of this bond. The subscription agreement is between the issuer and the managers and sets out the conditions under which the managers buy the bonds. The fiscal agency agreement covers the agent who handles payments. Legal opinions and a comfort letter from the issuer's auditors support the offer.

## Listing

Many bonds are listed on a regulated market or exchange, because some investors can only buy listed securities. The listing application is prepared by legal counsel or a listing agent. It is tracked in the checklist but does not block the move to the next stage.

## Exit criteria

The prospectus or programme update is approved, the draft final terms are ready, the subscription agreement is agreed and the legal opinion and comfort letter arrangements are confirmed. These tasks block the move to Pre-marketing.
