# Glossary

Short definitions of the terms used in the new-issue process and in this application.

## Deal roles

A bookrunner is a bank that markets the bond, collects orders and helps allocate it. The lead managers are the bookrunners that run the deal for the issuer. DCM, short for Debt Capital Markets, is the team that manages the issuer relationship and originates mandates. The syndicate desk executes the deal: it runs the order book, advises on pricing and proposes the allocation.

## Pricing terms

The spread is the extra yield over a reference rate such as mid-swaps. Initial price thoughts are the first spread indication given to investors, and guidance is the narrowing range that follows. The coupon is the interest rate paid on the bond. The new issue concession is the small extra yield offered to investors for buying a new bond instead of an existing one. The tenor is the time until the bond matures.

## Order book terms

The cover ratio, or oversubscription, is total orders divided by the deal size. Real money means long-term investors such as asset managers, insurers and pension funds. A scale-back reduces orders when the book is larger than the deal. Price tension is demand strong enough that the spread can be tightened during the book.

## Documents and identifiers

Final terms are the document that sets out the terms of one bond issued under a programme. An EMTN programme is a Euro Medium Term Note programme, a framework for issuing many bonds. The ISIN is the 12-character international identifier of a security and ends with a check digit. The four-eyes principle means that one person prepares an action and another person approves it.

## About this playbook

The playbook in this demo is a generic, illustrative description of a new-issue process written for a portfolio project. All deals, issuers and investors in the application are synthetic. It does not describe the policy of any bank.
