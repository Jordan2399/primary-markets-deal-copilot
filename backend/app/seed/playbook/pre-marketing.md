# Pre-marketing

Pre-marketing is the preparation before the bond is announced. The syndicate desk decides when to go, tests investor appetite and prepares the first pricing view.

## Choosing the issuance window

Issuers usually avoid issuing close to publishing their results, because inside information makes marketing difficult. These quiet periods are called blackouts. The syndicate desk also checks public holidays, the calendar of other issuers, major economic events and general market volatility. A good window has stable markets and enough investor attention. Checking the window is a blocking task owned by syndicate.

## Soundings and roadshows

Syndicate and DCM may sound out a few important investors to learn where they see the spread, which size they could support and which tenors they prefer. For a first-time issuer or a complex structure the issuer may also hold meetings with investors, often called a roadshow. Feedback is logged so it can be used later when allocating.

## Initial price thoughts

Initial price thoughts, or IPTs, are the first indication of the spread at which the bond may price. They are normally given as an area, for example a spread over mid-swaps. The desk builds them from the issuer's existing bonds, comparable issuers and a new issue concession, which is the extra yield investors expect for buying a new bond.

## Approvals

Internal commitment approvals and compliance sign-off on the marketing material are obtained before launch. If any investor has been given inside information, the compliance rules for that situation apply.

## Exit criteria

The window is confirmed, the sales desks are briefed with initial price thoughts prepared, and the internal approvals are in place.
