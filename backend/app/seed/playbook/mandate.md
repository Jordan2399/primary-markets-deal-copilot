# Mandate

The mandate stage is where an issuer chooses its banks and agrees the broad shape of a bond. It ends when the issuer has signed a mandate letter and the deal team knows what it is building.

## What happens at mandate

The DCM marketer discusses the issuer's funding needs: currency, size range, tenor and format, for example a senior unsecured benchmark or a green bond. The issuer appoints its bookrunners (the lead managers who run the deal). The mandate letter records each bank's role and the basic commercial terms. The syndicate team joins early to give a view on market capacity, investor demand and timing.

## Compliance and onboarding

Know-your-customer checks, sanctions screening and conflict checks are completed before the bank commits to the mandate. A deal does not move forward without them. In the task checklist this is a blocking task owned by compliance.

## Ratings

The issuer's credit ratings are confirmed with the rating agencies. For a new programme or an unusual structure the agencies may need to be briefed, because the rating affects pricing and which investors are allowed to buy the bond.

## Exit criteria

The deal leaves the Mandate stage when the mandate letter is signed, the structure (currency, tenor, format) is agreed and onboarding is complete. Ratings confirmation and staffing the team are tracked but do not block the move.
